You've finished [your resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/). Your education, skills, and work experience are all in place. But the document still doesn't look right. Maybe the text feels crowded, or the font looks too casual. You want your resume to look professional without spending hours fixing its design.

The good news is that you don't need a fancy font. **Arial, Calibri, and Aptos** are reliable choices for most job applications. They look clean and are easy to read. Here are 10 professional options to help you choose the right one.

## What Is the Best Font for a Resume?

**Arial is one of the safest choices for a resume.** Its simple letters are easy to read, and its familiar appearance suits almost every industry. It's a practical option for freshers, experienced professionals, and students applying for internships.

Prefer a slightly different look? Calibri has a softer appearance, while Aptos offers a more modern style. Both work well for professional documents. Choose the one that keeps your resume clear and readable.

## 10 Best Fonts for a Resume Recommended 

Not sure which font to pick? Start with these 10 options. Each offers a different look for your job application.

| Font | Best For |
|---|---|
| Arial | General job applications |
| Calibri | Office and corporate jobs |
| Aptos | Modern business resumes |
| Helvetica | Minimal resume layouts |
| Garamond | Academic and writing roles |
| Georgia | Education and research |
| Times New Roman | Formal applications |
| Lato | Marketing and communication |
| Roboto | IT and technology jobs |
| Cambria | Finance and business |

### 1. Arial - Best for General job applications

Arial keeps your resume simple and professional. Its letters remain clear at smaller sizes. Use it for office jobs, internships, and corporate applications.

### 2. Calibri - Best for Office and corporate jobs

Calibri has a softer appearance than Arial. It works well for resumes with detailed work experience. Choose it if you prefer a modern, familiar style.

### 3. Aptos - Best for Modern business resumes

Aptos is a contemporary Microsoft Office font. Its clean appearance suits organized resume layouts. It's a good option if you want something newer than Calibri.

### 4. Helvetica - Best for Minimal resume layouts

Helvetica has a clean, understated design. It suits business, marketing, and technology applications. Check its availability before sharing your document.

### 5. Garamond - Best for- Academic and writing positions

Garamond has a traditional appearance. It can make a text-heavy CV look refined. Keep the font size comfortable so your experience remains easy to read.

### 6. Georgia - Best for Education and research

Georgia has distinctive letter shapes and a traditional style. It's a good option for teaching, research, and publishing applications.

### 7. Times New Roman - Best for Formal applications

Times New Roman is familiar in academic and professional documents. It suits traditional industries and formal CVs. Avoid shrinking it too much to fit extra information.

### 8. Lato - Best for Marketing and communication

Lato looks modern without being flashy. It works well for marketing, sales, and customer-facing roles. Use it when you want a slightly distinctive style.

### 9. Roboto - Best for IT and technology jobs

Roboto has a clean, digital appearance. It's suitable for software developers, IT professionals, and digital marketers. Its simple design keeps technical information easy to scan.

### 10. Cambrian - Best for Finance and formal careers

Cambria is designed for comfortable reading in longer documents. Its traditional style suits business, finance, and education applications.

| Job Type | Recommended Fonts |

| Marketing and sales | Lato, Aptos |

For creative roles, a modern font can complement your portfolio. For government and corporate applications, a familiar font is a practical choice.

## What Is the Best Font Size for a Resume?

Even a good font can look bad when the size is wrong. Text that's too small is difficult to read. Oversized headings take up space you may need for your experience.

Use these sizes as a starting point:

| Resume Section | Recommended Size |
|---|---:|
| Your name | 20–24 pt |
| Section headings | 12–14 pt |
| Main content | 10–12 pt |
| Contact details | 10–11 pt |

For most resumes, 11 pt works well for the main text. Keep headings slightly larger so readers can find each section quickly.

If your resume doesn't fit, remove outdated or irrelevant details before reducing the font size.

## Which Fonts Are Best for an ATS-Friendly Resume?

When you apply for a job online, your resume may go through an Applicant Tracking System (ATS). This software helps employers organize and review applications.

You may have heard that an ATS rejects resumes because of their fonts. That's not quite how it works. No particular font guarantees selection or rejection. However, unusual fonts and complicated formatting can make text harder to extract.

Arial, Calibri, Aptos, and Times New Roman are practical choices. Follow these formatting tips:

- Use familiar fonts that are widely supported.
- Keep headings simple, such as Experience, Education, and Skills.
- Avoid decorative fonts and image-based text.
- Don't place important details inside complicated text boxes.
- Follow the employer's instructions for Word or PDF files.

A simple document structure helps both software and recruiters find your information.

Your resume doesn't need an unusual [font to stand out](https://aestheticfonts.in/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-font-style/). Choose a readable option, use consistent sizes, and give your qualifications the attention they deserve.

> **For most job applications, Arial at 11 pt is a reliable starting point.**

## Unprofessional Fonts to Avoid on a Resume or CV

Comic Sans MS, Papyrus, Curlz MT, and Brush Script may look creative, but they can make your resume appear less professional. Their decorative letters can distract from your skills and work experience. Avoid handwriting fonts, unusual symbols, and overly stylized text in your CV, especially when applying for corporate or office jobs. Stick to a clean font that makes your qualifications easy to read.
